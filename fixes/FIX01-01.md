**Date:** 10 October 2026.

**Status:** Phase 0 complete: comparison semantics, ownership rules and the
coordinated amendment plan are defined. Phases 1–4 remain outstanding; no runtime
change or improved model outcome is claimed.

**Scope:** [FIX01 R6 follow-up item 1](FIX01.md#proposed-recovery-follow-up-and-implementation-order).

FIX01 remains the active backlog; this document details the rollout of that one
item. Phase 0 was completed under the user's explicit instruction to implement
that phase on 10 October 2026. Its decisions below define the approved design scope
for the later coordinated implementation; they do not authorize executing those
later phases. Accepted runtime contracts remain unchanged until that cutover,
and the overall design remains **Proposed design**. No new repair permission,
verdict reassessment or live call is authorized. Prior completed work and
experiments remain in [Z_FIX01](Z_FIX01.md).

## Outcome and boundaries

Replace the model's independent culprit selection with assessments of comparisons
bound by native domain code. The existing loss call assesses preservation; one
native owner validates those assessments and derives attribution. Existing repair
owners separately decide whether the attributed defect has an authorized repair.

The intended improvement is fewer unjustified upstream rebuilds while retaining
the ability to identify genuine upstream loss. Valid JSON, complete comparison
answers and valid citations do not establish that the model's judgments are correct.

Keep one cohesive loss call. Do not add a reviewer, a call per producer, a generic
semantic dependency graph, model-specific branches, keyword-based attribution or
parallel production attribution policies. Do not change authoring, exact-reference
permissions, entity decisions, role/summary repair permissions or retry allowances
as part of item 1. Do not reinterpret JSON-looking prose as an exact reference.

## Evidence and feasibility

The [latest inspected E2E](../zig-out/e2e-spec/2026-10-10T03-24-03Z-2a2774d40362927ff90dc560ad009d3a/report.md)
introduced defective description text, attributed it to extraction, rebuilt
upstream work and reproduced the defect. Its final loss response also selected an
invalid source range. The [completed 16-call comparison](../test/calibration/source-loss-attribution/README.md)
failed its semantic quality gate. Those results motivate this candidate; they do
not validate it. Previous live allowances are consumed.

The engineering change is feasible within existing domain owners. The principal
risks are incorrect comparison scope, confusing absence with introduced loss,
mistaking provenance for semantic causality, and increasing response complexity
without improving judgments. It is a coordinated evidence-contract change, not
just a prompt edit or a generic-engine rewrite.

Three code facts constrain the design:

- [Authoring input](../src/domain/specification_session.zig) resolves original
  extracted claims and sources. [Role bindings](../src/domain/specification_source_binding.zig)
  select those claims; signal prose does not replace their requirement text.
  A defective signal paraphrase does not by itself establish why authoring failed.
- [Current omission findings](../src/domain/specification_support_model.zig)
  contain source IDs and free-text detail, not atomized business obligations.
  Native code cannot silently split a compound finding into new semantic tasks.
- [Current producer projection](../src/domain/source_omission_context.zig)
  omits generation roles, while [loss locations](../src/domain/source_omission.zig)
  have no role-selection or summary repair target. Better diagnosis must not
  manufacture a repair permission for those owners.

## Phase 0 — Define the comparison contract

**Complete — 10 October 2026.** This is the decision record for Phases 1–2, not
a claim that their code or tests exist. The reviewed owners are linked below.
The generic runner remains capability-free; model-assisted support assessments
remain distinct from native validation and permission checks.

### 0.1 One fixed finding and native-bound evidence views

Use the existing finding identity, reviewed subject/slot, source IDs, detail and
current authority/revision as one immutable scope. Do not mint a new semantic
obligation, split its prose, reopen its verdict or widen its source authority.
The detail describes the alleged missing meaning; the selected original sources
remain its evidence. Conditions, negation, triggers and obligation strength are
part of that meaning, not optional wording to normalize away.

Native code binds **evidence views** for the relevant producer inputs and outputs.
A view is a complete, identified value or collection assessed for that fixed
meaning and purpose. Each model-assessed view is one comparison assignment.
Native code also binds which input/output views belong to a producer and which
actual output supplies a consumer's required input. The model cannot select those
relationships or repair targets. These are domain facts, not a new runner graph.

Each binding retains:

- The finding, subject, purpose and current source/reference/candidate dependencies.
- The producer and side being assessed, complete member occurrences, resolved
  values, assignment eligibility and any unchanged supporting context.
- Source evidence with original occurrence identity and selectable span references.
- Whether a value supplies assigned meaning, controls assignment, or is only
  contextual evidence. Citation links and invalidation dependencies alone do not
  establish a content dependency.

Scope is built from the finding's source IDs, reviewed target and existing canonical
claim/group/role relationships. Include all chunks of the selected sources when
there is no extracted claim to narrow the search. Close related groups over their
complete membership; additional members remain evidence, not permission to widen
the fixed finding. Include actual target assignments even when their provenance
differs from what the defective output selected. Reconstruct completed-unit facts
through current canonical owners, never a consumed authoring cursor or a log lookup.

Use one shared view when the exact finding, purpose, member occurrences, values and
dependency binding are equal. Similar text is insufficient. Supporting text cannot
be counted as the assessed output: showing the original claim beside a deficient
signal must not make that signal appear preserved. Likewise, original sources
visible as context do not turn an unassigned requirement into an authorized
authoring obligation. Eligible exact occurrences remain separate available choices;
their visibility neither proves behavioral meaning nor expands repair permission.

Joint support is assessed as a collection at the boundary responsible for that
collection. Do not require each member to express the entire finding. If a compound
finding spans independently owned parts and the available bindings cannot establish
one responsible producer, retain unresolved ownership. This deliberately permits
an inconclusive result rather than inventing obligation decomposition or group
repair. No empty catalogue or missing historical facts count as preservation.

### 0.2 Resolve inherited absence with separate input/output assessments

**Decision:** retain the three response labels, but apply them to individual bound
evidence views. A single verdict for a whole producer cannot distinguish introduced
loss from inherited absence. A producer's attribution is derived from its input
and output assessments; it is not another model-returned verdict.

| Model result for one assigned view | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `preserved` | The complete fixed meaning within the assigned purpose is represented by this view, including necessary conditions and strength. Valid paraphrases and joint support are allowed. |
| `lost` | Identifiable required meaning is absent or materially weakened in this view. This label describes absence at a boundary, **not** a claim that this producer caused it. |
| `uncertain` | The supplied view/evidence does not support a definite assessment, including ambiguous scope or competing interpretations. |

Return exactly one result and supporting evidence per assigned comparison ID.
Input and output may require separate results in the same existing loss call;
shared identical views are assessed once. Native binding supplies all fixed IDs
and associations. The response has no independently selected culprit, owner,
repair operation, dependency list or copied obligation definition.

Each result carries selected source spans, references to assessed members where
present, and a concise explanation. Permit multiple source spans and multiple
members for joint support. `preserved` identifies where the meaning is expressed;
`lost` identifies the absent/changed meaning and the complete assessed output;
`uncertain` identifies what cannot be established. An empty assessed collection
has no member to cite, so its binding plus source evidence supplies the negative
comparison. Do not require a fabricated output citation for absence. Existing
source-selection validation governs spans; annotations outside the offered source
or view membership reject. A fluent explanation is never native semantic proof.

The reviewed subject's deficient output is fixed by the admitted omission and is
not reassessed. Native code binds that premise without fabricating a model result.
Original source support asserted by the fixed finding is likewise an existing
premise for its source collection, not a fresh approval of the verdict or proof
that every individual chunk contains the whole meaning. Smaller source/input
views still require their assigned assessments. Missing native evidence for either
fixed premise rejects preparation. The model may report uncertainty in other views
but cannot withdraw the finding or declare the reviewed output supported.

| Input view | Output view | Native interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `preserved` | `preserved` | No loss at this boundary. |
| `preserved` | `lost` | Introduced loss at this boundary, subject to responsibility and dependency checks in §0.4. |
| `lost` | `lost` | Meaning was already absent at input; this pair establishes no new culprit. Trace an established upstream loss or leave ownership unresolved. |
| `lost` | `preserved` | Meaning is represented again at output; do not propagate the earlier absence past this view or treat this as a repair success. |
| `uncertain` | Any result | No introduced-loss conclusion from this pair. |
| Any result | `uncertain` | No introduced-loss conclusion from this pair. |

Partial absence counts as `lost` only when the missing part is identifiable within
the assigned scope. Several independently missing parts may prevent unique
attribution even when each view's result is definite. The table does not infer
which clauses are missing from matching `lost` labels.

Signal prose is not the requirement text copied into authoring. If the actual
authoring assignment preserves the meaning, a defective signal paraphrase cannot
be blamed for the candidate's omission. It can be a separate defect when reviewed
in its own scope. Similarly, a role decision can be wrong even when all producer
prose retains the words. Original sources offered as corroboration do not bypass
the native assignment contract.

### 0.4 Native attribution and permission decision table

Validate the complete response first, then derive attribution for **one finding**.
Work backwards from its reviewed target through native-bound input relationships.
At a boundary with complete supported input and deficient output, attribute that
boundary; do not continue upstream merely to find a selectable repair location.
For deficient input, consider upstream boundaries that actually supply/control
that input. Supporting-context defects alone are not causality. A consumer's
complete assigned input cuts off propagated absence, even if other context is bad.

For a composite input, require assessment of the complete collection. Attribute an
upstream producer only when the native bindings and admitted assessments establish
one responsible boundary for this fixed loss. Mere shared claim IDs, matching
`lost` labels, array order or a broad invalidation relationship are insufficient.
If the supplied view granularity cannot distinguish partial causes, return
unresolved attribution rather than introduce finer semantic groups natively.

| Condition | Attribution | Repair consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Missing/duplicate/foreign assessment, invalid span/member, forbidden field or structurally contradictory fixed binding | Invalid comparison response | No derived target; existing protocol correction where applicable, otherwise native rejection remains terminal. |
| Source, target, producer, assignment or dependencies stale/mismatched | Invalid native assignment/evidence | Rebuild through the existing invalidation path or fail; no model correction of stale authority. |
| Target's assigned input is preserved; fixed target output is deficient | Target producer | Use its existing authorization only. Candidate attribution does not itself grant any reference addition. |
| Target input lacks meaning; one current upstream boundary demonstrably introduces this loss on its actual supply path | That upstream producer | Run the existing exact target/dependency/permission checks. Rebuild only if they authorize it. |
| Meaning absent at both sides of a downstream boundary | No new owner from that boundary | Do not blame the downstream producer for inherited absence; evaluate independently established upstream evidence. |
| Assignment omits supported meaning while eligible upstream evidence retains it | Role/assignment owner when uniquely established | Explicit unsupported repair outcome under this item; no relabelling as extraction or candidate loss. |
| Known owner but unavailable atomic operation, coupled target outside permission, or unrepairable summary/role/entity-decision scope | Known owner, unsupported repair | Block/fail through existing typed non-success; do not broaden permission or fall back to another culprit. |
| Necessary uncertainty, competing paths/owners, inseparable compound finding, empty relevant catalogue or unavailable producer history | Unresolved attribution | No upstream rebuild. Preserve independently authorized safe local repair checks without calling the diagnosis candidate loss; otherwise explicit failure. |
| Uncertainty or a defect in supporting-only context, while the target's complete input/output establish its loss | Target attribution remains available | Do not let unrelated semantic uncertainty hide a separately authorized repair. All required response entries still must be structurally valid. |
| Several independent admitted findings | Derive each separately | Existing continuation may select an authorized target; unrelated unresolved findings are not cleared and still prevent successful publication. |

Duplicate assessments of the same bound view reject even if their words agree.
Use one assessment for shared views so native code need not reconcile competing
answers for identical evidence. Explanations contradicting their labels remain a
semantic quality defect for evaluation; native code must not claim to detect that
through string matching. Neither complete answers nor this derivation prove
entailment. No reassessment, voting or semantic retry is added.

### 0.5 Lifecycle decisions and required amendments

Keep one current comparison evidence record with the fixed finding. Retain stable
comparison occurrences within that immutable assignment, member/source selectors,
dependency facts and separate finding/comparison origins. Corrections reuse the
assignment and defect allowance; reordering answers changes neither identity nor
attribution. A rebuilt assignment invalidates the old evidence, not the existing
retry history for the same unresolved defect.

Persist enough canonical bindings to rederive any retained attribution without
request logs. Reconstruct views from captured canonical state where possible;
any necessary retained value must be checked against its native authority. If
execution-only history cannot support readback, it cannot become persisted repair
authority. Do not add checkpoints, log replay or a second provenance ledger.

The stored comparison shape changes, so Phase 2 must increment the current
`specification-state/v9` version and replace its reader/writer/fixtures together.
No dual reader, legacy culprit fallback or independently writable derived location
is retained. Failed-run diagnostic evidence stays diagnostic; it does not become
published success or a resumable repair receipt.

## Phase 3 — Compare semantic outcomes

**Purpose:** test whether the more explicit assignment improves decisions rather
than merely producing a larger, valid response.

1. Review baseline case premises against actual production inputs and assignments.
   In particular, the current reconciliation-condition fixture demonstrates defective
   signal prose; it does not establish that authoring consumed that prose instead
   of the complete original claim. Distinguish producer-defect detection from
   causally attributing a candidate defect. Preserve historical results and record
   any revised case/label as new evidence rather than rewriting the failed baseline.
2. Freeze paired requests and their respective response contracts. Hold source facts,
   fixed findings, model/settings and transport constant within each pair. Record
   hashes, full requests, repetitions, total permitted sends, assessment criteria
   and failure thresholds before dispatch. Compare the contract change as a bundle;
   do not attribute any gain to one prompt sentence without an isolating experiment.
3. Include the captured candidate defect, unrelated candidate defects, genuine
   extraction loss, genuine loss at another supported boundary, inherited absence,
   joint/multi-source support, wrong assignments and unresolved ownership. Separate
   development cases from previously unused cases. Establish initial quality on a
   fixed configuration before broader model/reasoning comparisons.
4. Use existing diagnostic/replay tooling after fresh bounded approval. Do not add
   production shadow execution, automatic judges or unapproved retries. Validate
   new responses through the production native admission owner as well as schema
   checks; when replay cannot do that, report native admission as unassessed and
   obtain separate evidence before accepting the mechanics for those responses.
5. Score false upstream attribution, false candidate attribution, missed genuine
   loss, uncertainty, evidence adequacy and unsupported allegations separately.
   Include completion/truncation, schema/native rejection, actual tokens and
   latency. Count every dispatched trial; an always-uncertain candidate does not
   pass by avoiding upstream blame. An attributed defect is not an executed repair.

**Gate:** the candidate meets the predeclared safety, detection and completion
criteria and demonstrates an improvement over baseline without hiding regressions
in aggregate scores. State sample limits. A failed or inconclusive gate leaves
semantic acceptance open: revise the candidate and freeze a new comparison before
seeking another bounded run. Do not compensate with wider permissions or more judges.

## Phase 4 — Verify production recovery and publication

**Purpose:** prove that improved diagnosis results in useful, authorized recovery.

First use offline production integration paths to verify attribution, target
authorization, repair, affected validation, full validation and continuation. Cover
both candidate-local and genuine upstream repairs; verify unrelated work and retry
identities survive as required. Keep these tests separate from live E2E.

Then prepare explicit live cases and obtain approval for each E2E invocation under
[§28](../design/contracts/28-testing.md). Exercise the complete production path and
grade its actual published output with the required rubric. Include the motivating
case and unrelated requirements; genuine source gaps must retain their existing
clarification/blocking behavior. Report first-pass success, recovered success,
recurrence, unnecessary rebuilds, repair-induced harm and total cost separately.

Two independent follow-ups may prevent the captured failure from completing:

| Separate FIX01 item | Boundary to preserve |
| --- | --- |
| R6 follow-up item 2: exact-reference repair eligibility | The captured description never selected the required exact-token claim. Correct candidate attribution alone does not authorize adding it. Close the source-authorized permission contract separately, with occurrence, provenance, repair and readback tests. |
| R6 follow-up item 3: invalid diagnostic-evidence correction | Missing or invalid comparisons can still terminate after schema admission. A bounded correction transition needs its own explicit contract, fixed subject/finding, current evidence, unchanged allowance identity and negative tests. |

Do not silently include either change in item 1 or require both before recording its
offline/semantic results. If complete recovery depends on them, record that exact
dependency, implement only the authorized follow-up and verify its effects before
claiming end-to-end recovery. Attribute results from a combined candidate to the
combined changes, not to comparison binding alone.

**Gate:** real publication, complete required validation and rubric evaluation meet
the declared criteria. Early failure or an unrepairable correctly diagnosed defect
is useful evidence but does not pass this gate. No golden document or narrower
replay can substitute for complete production execution.
